3. Why Copy-Pasting & Multi-Line Entry Failed — All 7 Exact Root Causes & Fixes

Every behavior of the agent-city:~# prompt is governed by src/07_Execute_Actions/terminal/Terminal.tsx and src/07_Execute_Actions/terminal/commands.ts. Master these seven rules and no paste will ever fail again:

RULE A · MANDATORY PREFIX

Start Line 1 With noir

Root cause: commands.ts parses the first word of every buffer as a shell command. Pasting raw code like let x = 5; or // comment throws ash: let: not found or ash: //: not found.

Exact fix: Always begin Line 1 with lowercase noir + space: noir let x = 5; print(x);.

RULE B · NEVER REPEAT NOIR

Write noir on Line 1 ONLY

Root cause: 'noir' belongs to the RAW command set in commands.ts. Every character after the first noir —including all newlines, quotes, and comments—goes intact to NoirSession.run(). Writing noir again on line 2 causes a Crimson Noir Script parse error!

Exact fix: Prefix Line 1 only when pasting a block (only prefix every line if you submit them one-by-one with Enter between each).

RULE C · MULTI-LINE ENTRY

Desktop Shift+Enter vs. Mobile 1-Line

Desktop (Win / macOS / Linux / Web / Tauri): Press Shift+Enter to add a newline without executing; the console renders a right-aligned … continuation prompt under agent-city:~# . Inside a multi-line script, Tab inserts 2 spaces, ↑/↓ move between lines (recalling history only on the first/last line), and plain Enter executes the entire block.

Touch (Android Chrome / iOS Safari): Mobile keyboards receive enterKeyHint="go" and lack Shift+Enter, so Return submits immediately. Use semicolon-separated single lines or tap Copy 1-Line (Mobile & Track A Safe) on every lab in this manual!

RULE D · HIDDEN TEXTAREA FOCUS

Solid Blinking Cursor + Keyboard Paste

Root cause: Right-click context menus are suppressed over the city canvas, and the prompt mirrors an invisible <textarea class="r0-input">. A hollow rectangular outline (data-idle) means the textarea is NOT focused.

4-Step Foolproof Flow: (1) Open console (~ or Touch Menu → Console), (2) Left-click the dark console background until the cursor becomes a solid blinking block, (3) Paste with Ctrl+V (Win/Linux), Cmd+V (Mac), or Long-Press → Paste (Phone/Tablet), (4) Press Enter.

RULE E · TRACK A ~ HIJACK

Typing ~ Closes Track A

Root cause: On Track A (fullscreen = false), isToggleKey() intercepts Backquote, `, and ~ during window capture and closes the console instead of typing bitwise NOT ~.

3 Exact Workarounds: (1) Use the bit-identical branchless XOR expression x ^ -1 (for i32) and x ^ 0xFFFFFFFFu (for u32), (2) Paste ~ from clipboard, or (3) Open Track B (?boot=tty), where fullscreen = true disables the toggle so ~ types normally!

RULE F · 8,192-CHAR BUFFER CAP

Stay Within MAX_CHARS = 8192

Root cause: Terminal.tsx sets maxLength={8192} on .r0-input, silently truncating anything past character 8,192.

Exact fix: Every Copy button in this ebook checks length <= 8192 and shows exact character count. Larger projects split cleanly into sequential pastes because NoirSession retains all fn and global let declarations across submissions!

ELI5 · 32 Light Switches and Three Pairs of Reading Glasses

Bolt 32 tiny light switches side by side on a board. In Crimson Noir Script, that board of 32 switches (4 bytes) is called one memory cell. The board itself holds nothing except 0s and 1s. Whether that pattern means -1, 4,294,967,295, or a floating-point decimal depends entirely on which pair of "reading glasses"—called a static type (i32, u32, or f32)—you tell the compiler to wear!

1. Inside One 32-Bit Cell: u32, i32, and IEEE-754 f32

Crimson Noir Script allocates one contiguous ArrayBuffer of cells * 4 bytes and attaches three views—Int32Array, Uint32Array, and Float32Array—to the exact same physical bytes:

  • u32 (Unsigned Integer, 0u .. 4_294_967_295u): Pure binary counting with powers $2^0, 2^1, \dots, 2^{31}$. Arithmetic wraps modulo $2^{32}$ via hardware semantics (0u - 1u == 4294967295u). Supports decimal (42u), hex (0xFFu), binary (0b1010u), and visual digit separators (1_000_000u).
  • i32 (Signed Two's Complement, -2_147_483_648 .. 2_147_483_647): Bit 31 contributes $-2^{31}$ while bits 30..0 contribute $+2^k$. Adding 1 to 2147483647 wraps cleanly to -2147483648; integer division truncates toward zero and catches division by zero as a safe named error ("division by zero").
  • f32 (IEEE-754 Single-Precision binary32): Packs 1 sign bit $s$, 8 biased exponent bits $E$ (bias 127), and 23 mantissa bits $M$ into 32 bits, representing $(-1)^s \times 2^{E-127} \times (1 + M/2^{23})$. Every single operation rounds through Math.fround (never silently keeping 64-bit precision) and formats via f32Text (the shortest 1..9 significant-digit decimal that round-trips identically, using exponent notation below 1e-6 and from 1e21). Remember: write 0.5 or 2.0, never leading-dot .5!
  • Zero Implicit Coercion, Smart Literal Adoption & Saturating Casts: Adding i32 + f32 is rejected at compile time with a helpful hint (use 'as f32' or 'as i32'), while unsuffixed integer literals smoothly adopt an adjacent f32 or u32 type (let x: f32 = 2;, x * 4, [1, 2.5]). Explicit f32 as i32 and f32 as u32 saturate cleanly at type bounds and turn NaN into 0.

WHY #1 Why does a wrong branch guess waste 15 to 20 cycles when a simple math op takes 1 cycle? >

Out-of-order superscalar CPUs fetch, decode, rename, and speculatively execute dozens of micro-ops past a conditional jump before the comparison result retires. On a misprediction, the Reorder Buffer (ROB) must squash every speculative instruction down the wrong path and refill the entire front-end pipeline from the corrected program counter—burning ~15 cycles on Apple M-series and ~18–20 cycles on modern x86 cores.

WHY #2 Why is b ^ ((a ^ b) & m) safer than (a & m) | (b & ~m)? >

First, it needs only 3 bitwise operations instead of 4 (one XOR, one AND, one XOR—no complement needed, so it doesn't even need ~m!). Second, over Boolean algebra $\mathbb{F}_2^{32}$, $b \oplus ((a \oplus b) \wedge m) = (a \wedge m) \oplus (b \wedge \neg m)$ holds identically on every bit lane, including raw IEEE-754 sign, exponent, and mantissa bits.

WHY #3 How does x & (-x) magically isolate the single lowest 1-bit inside ctz(x)? >

Write any nonzero word as $x = \alpha \, 1 \, 0^k$ (some prefix $\alpha$, a 1 at bit $k$, and $k$ trailing zeros). Flipping all bits gives $\neg x = (\neg \alpha) \, 0 \, 1^k$. Adding 1 carries through all $k$ trailing ones and stops right at bit $k$: $-x = (\neg \alpha) \, 1 \, 0^k$. Now AND them: $\alpha \wedge \neg \alpha = 0$, $1 \wedge 1 = 1$, $0^k \wedge 0^k = 0^k$—leaving purely $2^k$! Then $31 - \text{clz}(2^k) = k$.

WHY #4 How does SWAR popcount count 32 bits in 12 operations without a single loop? >

Treat one 32-bit word as sixteen 2-bit counters (v = v - ((v >>> 1) & 0x55555555)), sum adjacent pairs into eight 4-bit counters (0x33333333), sum nibbles into four 8-bit byte totals (0x0F0F0F0F), and multiply by 0x01010101—which adds all four bytes simultaneously into the top byte >>> 24!

WHY #1 Why didn't RAM speed up as fast as CPU math over the last 30 years? >

WHY #1 Why do LLMs turn words into continuous vectors instead of keeping integer IDs 1, 2, 3? >

WHY #1 Why do we divide Q . K by sqrt(d_k) (= 0.5 for d_k = 4) before running softmax? >

WHY #2 Why compute Q, K, and V with three separate matrices instead of just doing dot(x_t, x_j)? >

Separate $W_Q$ and $W_K$ let an adjective ask for a following noun ($q_{\text{adj}} \cdot k_{\text{noun}}$ high while $q_{\text{adj}} \cdot k_{\text{adj}}$ stays low), and separate $W_V$ chooses which attribute of that noun gets copied onto the residual stream.

WHY #3 Why does subtracting max(logits) leave every single softmax probability completely unchanged? >

Because $e^{z_i - m} = e^{z_i} \cdot e^{-m}$. The common positive factor $e^{-m}$ factors out of both numerator and denominator and cancels cleanly: $(e^{-m} e^{z_i}) / (e^{-m} \sum_j e^{z_j}) = e^{z_i} / \sum_j e^{z_j}$!

WHY #4 Why is 4 squarings of a degree-5 polynomial thousands of times more accurate than a degree-9 polynomial? >

Lagrange's remainder for degree 5 is $|r|^6 / 720$. Dividing $x$ by $16 = 2^4$ shrinks that remainder by $16^6 = 16,777,216\times$! Even after multiplying relative error by 16 during the 4 squarings, error drops by over a million-fold at the cost of just 4 extra multiplies!

WHY #5 Why can't a causal language model look at token t+1 during training or generation? >

During generation, token $t+1$ does not exist yet—predicting it is your entire job! During parallel training over a complete sentence, masking $j > t$ prevents position $t$ from trivially copying the answer already sitting at seat $t+1$, allowing all $T$ next-token predictions to train simultaneously in one matrix pass.

5-Whys Drill: Pre-Norm vs. Post-Norm, MLP Key-Value Memories & Autoregressive Factorization

WHY #1 Why apply RMSNorm BEFORE Attention and MLP (Pre-Norm) instead of after adding the residual (Post-Norm)? >

Xiong et al. (2020) proved that placing normalization on the side branch (h + Sublayer(RMSNorm(h))) keeps the main residual highway completely unnormalized and linear from Layer 0 to Layer $L$, giving well-behaved $O(1)$ gradients at step 0 without fragile learning-rate warmup. Every major LLM since GPT-2 uses Pre-Norm.

WHY #2 Why does a Transformer need an MLP at all when Attention already mixes tokens together? >

Given fixed attention weights $\alpha$, mixing values $\sum_j \alpha_{t,j} W_O W_V u_j$ moves information between seats linearly. Geva et al. (2021) showed MLP layers act as massive associative key-value knowledge stores: rows of $W_1$ detect patterns on the residual stream, ReLU switches on matching detectors, and columns of $W_2$ write recalled factual vectors back onto the stream.

WHY #3 Why read only the LAST position h[T-1] when predicting the next word? >

Because under causal masking, position $T-1$ is the unique seat whose attention mask [1, 1, 1, 1] allows reading every token $w_0, w_1, w_2, w_3$ in the prompt! Its final residual vector compresses the entire prompt's contextual state aimed at predicting $w_T$.

WHY #4 What is the difference between greedy argmax decoding and temperature sampling? >

Divide logits by temperature $\tau > 0$ before softmax: $p_v = \text{softmax}(\text{logit}_v / \tau)$. As $\tau \to 0^+$, all probability concentrates onto the single highest logit—exact deterministic argmax! Because Crimson Noir Script is strictly deterministic (zero hidden RNG by design), greedy decoding guarantees bit-identical trajectories across every machine (and deterministic sampling can be added cleanly using a morton2/rotl counter hash if desired).

WHY #5 Why does generating K new tokens take K forward passes (autoregression)? >

By the probability chain rule, any joint sequence probability factors exactly without approximation as $P(w_1, \dots, w_K \mid w_0) = \prod_{t=1}^K P(w_t \mid w_0, \dots, w_{t-1})$. Token $w_{t+1}$ depends causally on whichever concrete word $w_t$ was chosen a moment earlier—so the model feeds its own spoken output straight back into its input ear!

WHY #1 Why don't neural networks automatically put one concept per neuron during normal training? >

Because the real world contains millions of distinct concepts (bridges, rainstorms, traffic rules, jazz chords, syntax clauses), while a layer only has $d$ neurons! Because any single sentence only mentions a tiny handful of those concepts ($S \ll 1$), compressing $M \gg d$ nearly perpendicular directions into $\mathbb{R}^d$ achieves far lower prediction loss than throwing away $M - d$ concepts.

WHY #2 Wait—in 2D you only get 2 perpendicular axes! How can high dimensions hold way more than d almost-perpendicular directions? >

Concentrated measure! By the Johnson–Lindenstrauss Lemma, if you relax exact $90^\circ$ orthogonality ($|u \cdot v| = 0$) to tiny interference $|u \cdot v| \le \varepsilon$ (say $85^\circ$–$95^\circ$), $\mathbb{R}^d$ fits $\exp(c \, \varepsilon^2 d)$ unit vectors—exponentially many! Small interference noise below the ReLU threshold gets wiped clean to zero!

WHY #3 Why optimize L1 (sum of z_k) instead of directly differentiating L0 (popcount of active z_k)? >

Because $\|z\|_0$ is a flat staircase: its derivative is $0$ everywhere $z_k > 0$ and undefined at $0$, offering zero gradient signal to shrink weak features (and exact $L_0$ minimization is NP-hard). By Candès, Romberg & Tao (2006) and Donoho (2006), $L_1$ is the tightest convex relaxation of $L_0$, applying a constant inward force $-\lambda$ (proximal soft-thresholding) that drives non-essential features cleanly to exact $0.0$!

WHY #4 Why project the decoder gradient onto the tangent plane err - (err . d_k) * d_k before normalizing? >

Each dictionary atom $d_k$ lives on the unit sphere manifold $S^{d-1} = \{v \in \mathbb{R}^d : \|v\|_2 = 1\}$. Any radial component $(e \cdot d_k) d_k$ purely stretches or shrinks $d_k$'s length—only to be undone immediately by normalization, distorting effective step size (Bricken et al. 2023)! Subtracting the radial part gives the exact Riemannian gradient on $T_{d_k} S^{d-1}$.

WHY #1 Does splitting into H = 2 heads double the parameter count or matrix-multiply cost? >

WHY #2 Why change the scaling factor from 0.5 (1/sqrt(4)) to 0.70710678 (1/sqrt(2))? >
